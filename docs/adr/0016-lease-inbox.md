# Lease Inbox: one email address per Group, a classified inbox, work orders, and sending without a draft-approve loop

The landlord relationship is the roommate problem no other app touches: one person forwards (or forgets to forward) the inspection notice, the water shut-off, the rent change, and work orders die in someone's personal inbox. Lease Inbox gives the Group its own email address that the landlord can reach and that Roomie reads and writes.

Decisions:

1. **One inbox per Group**, provisioned through AgentMail when an Admin teaches the Trick ("roomie, learn lease inbox"). The address is said in the chat and shown on the dashboard. The pre-rebuild Kevin had the same shape (`kevin-4b@agentmail.to`, deleted in `4939d2c`) and is the reference.
2. **Connecting is three sentences, not a flow.** Roomie posts the address and says: forward your lease or the last landlord email to it; CC it next time you email them; or give it to the leasing office. Onboarding's wrap-up line, when the Trick is off, adds one sentence offering it.
3. **The landlord is learned, not configured.** When a Member forwards mail, the sender is the Member; Roomie parses the forwarded headers for the original sender and trusts that address and its **domain** as "the landlord". The intro email's recipient counts too. Two refinements from the build (#27): a forward teaches the landlord only when the forwarder is a **known Member** (their sign-in email matches; anyone else's forward is a new sender), and **public mail domains** (gmail.com, outlook.com and the like) are never trusted as a domain, only as the exact address. An **unknown sender** is filed under "new sender" and the Admin is asked once, privately: "someone@brookfield.com emailed about the parking lot: is that your building?" Yes trusts the domain. Only known senders can reach the chat, create reminders or touch work orders.
4. **Every inbound email is classified** and the class decides what happens: a **dated notice** (inspection, shut-off, maintenance window) gets one line in the chat, a Reminder the evening before, and is filed; a **rent or lease change** gets one line, a private note to the Admin, and a proposed change to the rent Expense that the Admin confirms (it's money); a **work-order reply** updates the work order and gets one line; **everything else** (promotions, events, digests) is filed silently and counted in the weekly report ("2 other emails"). A notice with a date inside 72 hours is urgent and uses the day's urgent slot (ADR 0017). Attachments (the lease, notices, bills) go to Documents (ADR 0018); lease and notice PDFs are read so "what's the notice period?" has an answer.
5. **Work orders are entities.** "The sink's leaking, tell them" creates one: what, who reported it, the thread, and a status that moves through sent → acknowledged → scheduled (with the date) → done, inferred from the landlord's replies. "What's up with the sink?" answers from it; the morning bundle mentions a visit the day before; after five quiet days Roomie offers to follow up.
6. **Sending has no draft-and-approve loop.** Any active Member can make Roomie email the landlord. A **simple** email sends at once and Roomie posts one line, "Sent: asked them to move the inspection to Thursday." Simple means: a reply in a thread the landlord started, or a new maintenance request; and the body commits to no dollar amount, no date the Member didn't say, and no lease or legal term (terminate, renew, notice, vacate, deposit, late fee, sublet). Anything else — a new recipient, money, lease terms, two Members contradicting each other within the hour — gets a question or two in the chat first, then sends. The rule is code, with tests; the model never decides whether to skip the check. (Since the #30 review: the service holds the asked-about send for ten minutes, bound to that Member, the recipient and the exact draft, and sends it only when that Member's own next message says yes or the dashboard returns the ask's token; a first "ok", someone else's yes or a changed draft gets the questions again.)
7. **Every email names the human.** The signature is two lines: "Sent by Roomie, the shared inbox for the residents of Apt 4B, at Maya Rao's request. Replies reach all residents." The requesting Member is always named; Roomie typed it, they said it. When a landlord address becomes known, Roomie offers (once) an **intro email** signed with every active Member's first name; a Member says "send it" or skips. No consent form, no portal.
8. **The dashboard inbox** shows the classes (Notices · Rent & lease · Work orders · Everything else · New senders) with a reply box under the same send rules and signature. Chat is the primary way to send; the page is for catching up and finding things.

## Consequences

- Lease Inbox requires the Composio-free AgentMail integration: a `MailClient` interface with the real client and a fake (the Splitwise / Instacart pattern), inbound through a webhook route that verifies the Svix signature on the raw body, answers 2xx at once, dedupes on `event_id` (deliveries are retried for ~27 hours and arrive out of order) and queues per Group. Attachments arrive as metadata and are fetched by `downloadUrl`; large bodies arrive as a `body_url`. One inbox per Group needs AgentMail's Developer plan (10 inboxes, +$2 a month each beyond); the operator's to-do list carries it.
- Forwarded mail is parsed by us: the original sender comes from the quoted `From:` block in `text` or `html` (Gmail and Outlook forwards are often HTML-only), with tests for both clients' formats. Mail that fails DMARC never arrives (dropped upstream) or arrives labelled `unauthenticated`, which the classifier treats as an unknown sender.
- The send rule, the classifier's post-processing, forwarded-header parsing and the work-order state machine are pure functions with tests. The model classifies and drafts; code decides what the class *does* and whether a draft may send.
- Forgetting the Trick stops reading and sending but keeps the inbox and documents; an Admin can release the address from Settings.
- "forget me" anonymizes a Member's name in sent-mail records and work orders; the emails themselves were sent and stay as they are.
- Rent changes never apply themselves; the Money Trick owns rent (ADR 0019) and the Admin confirms.

## Considered options

- **Members connect their own Gmail:** gives Roomie every bill automatically, but asks four people for mailbox access to solve a one-address problem. Parked as an optional later integration; never required.
- **Draft in chat, Admin approves, then send:** the safe default, and exactly the back-and-forth that makes people stop using it. Rejected for simple mail; kept, as "a question or two", for the complex class.
- **Admin-only sending:** a leaking sink is everyone's problem and the Admin is away. Any active Member; the signature carries their name.
- **One shared Roomie address with routing by landlord domain:** fewer inboxes, but two buildings on the same management company's domain collide, and the address can't be said in a chat as "ours". Rejected.
